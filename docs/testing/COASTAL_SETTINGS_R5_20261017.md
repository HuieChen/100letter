# Dear Somewhere — Settings R5 review

This stage repairs the supplied Settings composition and its working controls. It preserves the original PDF/JPEG artwork, existing five-case campaign, authored courier frames and previous player saves. It does not certify the whole game against the user's final quality standard. R4 is historical, unpublished evidence; R5 supersedes it. The frozen [evidence manifest](coastal-settings_20261017-r5/evidence.json) records source fingerprints and 227 retained artifacts.

The latest human feedback explicitly requires a clear ripple after clicking water. This remains an active follow-up despite the 168 passing mechanical checks. The R5 checkpoint is being published before that next correction; click-wave readability and motion acceptance are not declared complete.

## What changed

- Chinese labels use a measured, centered column and their own typography sizes. Selectors, expanded rows and focus states share rounded corners. The return arrow has a reserved hit area, remains usable over an open dropdown and works alongside Esc. Text and controls are checked at 1280, 1920 and 2560 widths in both languages.
- Slider knobs retain solid white cores. Tracks now end underneath the knob centers instead of leaving a square blue protrusion at either extreme. The sensitivity track is shorter so its full thumb travel clears the diagonal postcard. These fixes address the user's actual crop locations rather than treating the postcard/rail collision as a panel-border problem. Godot draws the rail across the widget and the filled area up to the knob center; its line style also has endpoint growth. See the [official slider drawing code](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/blob/4.7/scene/gui/slider.cpp) and [line-style implementation](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/blob/4.7/scene/resources/style_box_line.cpp).
- Header tape uses source-specific white-matte recovery. PDF page 12 contains only the dark folded end; the bright tape is already part of page 13's pale-paper plate. Both actual pigments are sampled from the unchanged originals. Tape/paper mixed pixels remain opaque, while export-white residue is removed. Genuine pale paper and its blue-grey writing keep their previous rendering.
- A later user crop also exposed a genuine white export fringe on the blue card, including in the current R5 render. Page 14 is a single flat blue pigment, so only that plate now gets its own source-sampled matte. The cream postcard border remains original paper. Over an independent matching-blue background, mean edge error fell from 0.43554 to 0.00701 across 825 source-selected washed texels; opaque blue pixels were unchanged. Live white menu/knob pixels also stayed unchanged at all three widths: 1,423 / 4,401 / 8,912 samples at 1280 / 1920 / 2560.
- The original painted water texture remains the basis of motion. Speed-dependent swipes, held drags and taps have distinct responses; original lemon shadow/water-arc pixels follow the real slice pose while farther ink stays fixed. The user rejected the perceived motion, so it was recalibrated in world pixels rather than exaggerated UV offsets. The new focused checks pass, but perceived realism and aesthetic acceptance are still pending. It remains a bounded illustrated field coupled to isolated Godot 2D bodies, not a general fluid simulation.
- Lemon down/up has pressure, displacement, a small rebound and different center/edge torque. Paper and controls stay fixed. Held input releases on outside up, Esc, return, focus loss, window exit and language rebuild; rebuild keeps physical position without retaining pointer capture. The lemon-specific edge material preserves cream flesh and pith.
- Display dropdowns close before applying native changes. Deferred requests coalesce to the latest values, and identical native window assignments are skipped. Preference write failure preserves the previous file and current in-memory values; a short localized status remains visible, and returning from Settings is still possible.
- Audio teardown explicitly stops this host's players while the mixer is alive, releases owned stream references and waits on a bounded playback-retirement fence before quitting. It does not stop another owner's voice or remove shared buses. This repairs lifecycle handling; it is not a human listening verdict.

## Failures investigated

The earlier display test reproduced a missing first-click dropdown after decoration changes. The repair removes the mutation from the button-release callback; the repeated-window-mode regression uses real routed clicks without retries. A separate geometry assertion originally confused scaled texture metadata with physical pixels. It now checks the decoded render image and retains the reported logical dimensions for diagnosis, rather than weakening the requested physical-resolution check.

At 17:17:27, the display fixture reported 544 checks and five failures. Event diagnostics showed a genuine native `focus_exited` between mouse down and up, rather than an unopened dropdown being fixed by a retry. The QA fixture now establishes its intended no-focus Window flag before Host startup; the 17:34:34 rerun passed 547 checks. Production focus-loss cancellation is preserved. The separate incomplete 17:07:19 run lacks that event trace and remains an unresolved historical failure record; it is not retroactively assigned the later diagnosis.

An intermittent ceramic-clue approach was accepted, then cancelled by a delayed focus-out in an intentionally non-focusable test window. Initial focus now comes from the actual Window. Such windows preserve accepted mouse approaches while rejecting keyboard movement; ordinary focusable game windows still cancel on focus loss. The native flag check runs with an actual renderer because the headless backend does not implement that Window flag. No clue state is injected or click retried to hide this failure.

The companion-water comparison initially exposed a mismatch between physics, rendered sprite pose and captured shader state. The corrected test observes completed GPU frames and compares independently transformed source pixels, not a duplicate of the production shader's inverse mapping. It checks translation, rotation, old-position ghosting and unchanged far ink.

The reopened water calibration uses a three-world-pixel displacement cap with gain 4.8. Its first new test reported 159 checks and five failures: native mass contrast, click displacement bound, click visibility, held-wake source correspondence and held-wake displacement bound. That failed run is retained. The final focused run at 18:00:56 passed 168 checks after correcting native-float mass comparison and separating genuinely observable image motion from straight-line tracking ambiguity. The native masses remain 3.3 and 1.25; no artwork is replaced to make the test pass.

The water proof has three distinct scopes:

- **Input-rate consistency:** the production solver replays the same 123.7-world-pixel path with 30, 60 and 144 events over one simulated second, at three clock/grid phases, with and without held dragging. Relative energy is 0.99028–1.01202, absolute-height weight is 0.99692–1.00227, maximum centroid difference is 1.826 world pixels, and minimum field cosine similarity is 0.97373. These are simulated event-rate comparisons, not measured display FPS. Independent initial-contact centroid error is at most 0.145 world pixels. Native impulse unit trials produce speeds 0.70493–0.71171 before a shared physics tick; they are not continuous real-time gestures.
- **Actual original-art response:** real routed click and held-wake input changed 17 and 9 readable source patches respectively; paper, labels and controls stayed fixed. Click tracking has only two unique correspondences, with 14 straight-line aperture rejections and one ambiguous match, so its broader original-source displacement remains explicitly unobservable. Held-wake tracking has six unique correspondences, three aperture rejections and no observed out-of-bound match. Ambiguous motion is not counted as a measured pass.
- **Independent displacement bounds:** isolated native-resolution plates reuse the actual production material and the GUI-driven field with a nonperiodic mathematical QA texture. Dense RGB correspondence finds 51 unique click features and 40 unique wake features, with maximum observed shifts of 3.005 and 2.636 world pixels. There are zero out-of-bound matches against the three-world-pixel contract plus 0.6-world-pixel raster-sampling tolerance. This supplementary texture is never registered or displayed as game art and does not replace the mandatory original-art visibility checks. The test does not copy the shader's gradient or mapping formula. Temporarily frozen bodies isolate refraction from moving companions.
